## 4. Room signature: in or out

**What is being decided** (PRD §6 S1, Q7; D-010, D-021): the room's features set the key root, the mode and the echo. The ripple sweeps table, then wall, then ceiling, ringing a chord, and the garden re-tunes. The Wanderer tuning takes over whenever the bed is at level 3 or 4 or a feature is unknown (TC-SIG-04). Everything is built and merged; unit TC-SIG-01..04, `17-ripple`, `29-room-matrix` 6/6 and `22-save-restore` all pass. **None of it has seen a real room.** T-129, the evidence this decision was meant to rest on, has not run.

**The case for in:**
1. It is the one hook without known prior art (D-010). The rubric rewards it by name: *"Purposeful passthrough: the real room meaningfully changes the experience"* (Experience) and *"scene understanding … room geometry"* (Technical, Innovation). Without it the pitch is a garden that sings, and that space has close, named antecedents (WINNING_STRATEGY §0.1).
2. It fails safe by construction. Missing planes, missing labels or a declined permission all lead to the Wanderer tuning, and the full game still plays at every level. A wrong reading still plays in tune, because every mode is an anhemitonic pentatonic set (D-025). The worst case on a judge's Quest is therefore the "out" version plus a ripple whose captions are honest about what it read (D-060 (7)).
3. Keeping it costs no code. It adds no draws beyond the ripple (inside the 48), its first sound is inside 20 s in all six rooms, and its re-tune is spread across ticks (T-143). The one browser-side cost is a 5–7 ms echo swap, once, at session start in the largest rooms.

**The case for out, argued honestly:**
1. The headline claim is the least verified part of the build. We have never seen Quest Browser report a labelled table, wall or ceiling. The Technical rubric's *"holding up in rooms the developer never tested"* is exactly what we cannot show.
2. A judge plays in one room, so they cannot hear that two rooms differ. The difference reaches them only through the ripple's captions and the Devpost text, and with no device run that text has to carry caveats ("not yet on a headset"), which blunts the pitch anyway.
3. About one room in five sits near a measuring step and may change scale on another day (item 21, unmeasured). A returning judge could read that as a bug.
4. "Out" is cheap now and gets dearer after the bug bash. It means forcing `WANDERER` in `room/` with one config switch, cutting the room captions, and rewriting the six marked Devpost sentences. That is about a day, or about an hour if the no-signature copy is written in advance.

**Recommendation: IN, with the Wanderer fallback and planes optional, as G1 said, plus four guards:**
1. The Devpost copy says exactly what was tested: the signature unverified on a headset, six emulated rooms placed and played, three fixture rooms giving three signatures by hand. The six `[not yet: T-155]` markers are replaced with honest wording, not removed.
2. The product-owner writes the **no-signature version** of the description now (DEVPOST_DRAFT §5: "add it to T-155"; tagline B and the video's version already exist), so that "out" costs an hour.
3. **Tripwires.** Any one of these before 10 Nov flips the signature to out at the bug bash (the Should rule, PRD §6): an S1 or S2 bug in the chain; a Playtest & React player hitting an error, a first sound past 20 s, or no ripple at all in a room with Space Setup; or a borrowed headset showing that planes or labels do not work in Quest Browser.
4. The evidence that would confirm "in" is G1 §4's list: DEVICE_SCRIPT_S1 blocks 1, 6, 7, 10, 15, 16, 17. Path A's screenshots are the partial version of it.
