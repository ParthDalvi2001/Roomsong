# Competition editions and verified winner directory — Meta Horizon Start / Meta VR Start Developer Competition and predecessors

Research date: 2026-10-10. Scope: every edition of the Start Developer Competition and its direct Meta/Oculus-run predecessors for Start members or on Devpost; full 2025 winner directory; judging rubrics; prize structures; submission counts; 2026 signals. Project context: Roomsong (hands-first MR music garden on a table, IWSDK + Three.js, Entertainment track / New Experience division, no headset on the team yet) — see `00_brief_and_required_deliverables.md`.

Method note (so the report writer can judge reliability): Devpost blocks proxy fetches (r.jina.ai returned 403 for most pages; the Wayback Machine is not reachable from this tool; the Chrome DevTools MCP browser profile was locked), but **direct HTTP fetches of Devpost pages succeeded**, so every 2025 winner page, both competition sites (overview, rules, judging, FAQ, webinars, special awards, dates, updates, forum, resources) and the 2024 predecessor gallery were downloaded and parsed locally. Meta developer blog posts were fetched directly. Meta Horizon Store pages returned only partial metadata (price, rating, genre) without input-mode or description fields. **YouTube videos could not be viewed**; only video titles/channels were retrieved via YouTube oEmbed, so "first 20 seconds" descriptions are not provided anywhere in this file. The shared web-search budget was exhausted mid-task, so secondary press coverage of the 2025 winners (UploadVR, Road to VR, XR Today, Mixed-News) could not be searched beyond what is cited.

## Key question 1 — Which editions exist, and which direct predecessors are verifiable?

### Takeaway
There are exactly two editions of the Start Developer Competition proper: the **Meta Horizon Start Developer Competition (2025; Devpost, entry 5 Nov–9 Dec 2025, $1.5M/32 awards)** and the **Meta VR Start Developer Competition 2026 (Devpost, entry 24 Sep–18 Nov 2026, $1M/20 awards)**. Meta explicitly frames 2026 as "the second" edition. The verifiable Meta-run predecessors are the **Presence Platform Hackathon 2023** (in-person, not Devpost) and the **Meta Quest Presence Platform Hackathon 2024** (Devpost, $30,985). I found no evidence of a "Meta Horizon Start Hackathon 2024" or any Devpost "Oculus Start hackathon" with winners.

### Inferences
- The 2025 and 2026 editions are the only two editions of this specific competition; the 2024 Presence Platform Hackathon is the closest predecessor in format (Devpost, MR-capability requirement, 3 categories), but it was community-judged by XR YouTubers with ~$31k in prizes, versus internal Meta judging and $1.5M in 2025 — so 2024 winners are weaker evidence of what Meta's own judges reward.
- Across 2023 → 2024 → 2025 → 2026, the requirement trend is monotonic: MR capability required (2024) → hand interactions and PCA rewarded as special awards (2025) → hands-first as a hard requirement plus seated/FoV guidance (2026).

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether a "Meta Horizon Start Hackathon 2024" ever existed; no source found (search for the phrase returned only 2025 content). Treat as non-existent unless the user has other evidence.
- Could not verify any "Oculus Start hackathon" on Devpost; search returned unrelated events. The Oculus Start program existed (Meta blog "What's New with Oculus Start"), but no competition winners list was found.
- 2021 XR Hackathon: platform and full Presence Platform winner list not extracted; only the first-place name and prize are cited.

## Key question 4 — Official judging criteria 2025 vs 2026 (and what changed)

### Takeaway
Both editions use the same four criteria at 25% each. 2026 keeps the wording but adds hard gates (hands-first end-to-end; seated-optimised; policy) and sharper rubric language: passthrough must be "purposeful", scene understanding/MRUK anchoring must "hold up in rooms the developer never tested", min 60 fps, FoV-aware design, gaze interactions, and the trailer must "show real gameplay" and not "lean on AI-generated video". 2025 judges were Meta-internal; 2026 adds third-party judges.

### Cited Findings
**2025 criteria (verbatim from the Devpost judging page):**
- "All submissions will be evaluated based on the equally weighted criteria below" — the rules page specifies 25% each. — [2025 judging](https://start-developer-competition.devpost.com/details/judging); [2025 rules](https://start-developer-competition.devpost.com/rules)
- Innovation & Creativity: "measures how original, creative, and relevant the concept is, and how it leverages Meta Horizon/Quest's unique capabilities. To score highly … the game or application should showcase a meaningful update or differentiate itself from what is currently available in the Meta Horizon Store. The idea should be original and drive repeat usage by players. It should be better supported by Meta Quest/Horizon vs. other devices or platforms."
- Experience Design: "Great experience design will lead the player to quickly grasp where to go and what to do, to find the fun or purpose easily and receive a fulfilling experience."
- Technical Implementation: "evaluates the technical expertise, reliability, and use of Meta tools/features … which include, but are not limited to, Passthrough Camera Access with AI and Hand Interactions. To score highly … should run at a minimum of 60 FPS on Quest 3/3S … minimal bugs and no gameplay blocking issues … Experiences should implement Meta tools and features."
- Polish & Presentation: "overall look and feel (UI/UX, art direction, sound design), attention to detail, completeness, and relevance to the competition track themes … The overall presentation of the project including the text description, images, and video quality meets the stated submission requirements."
- Judging process 2025: "we have selected a panel of experts from within Meta to be judges. There will be two stages of judging."
- 2025 track definitions ("Eyes on the Prize" update): Entertainment = "An experience that makes consuming or watching content more immersive, interactive, and innovative than traditional formats"; Lifestyle = "enhances peoples' daily lives, how they get things done, learn new skills, or connect with others around shared interests"; Social Game = "connects people in real time to play together online or in a colocated space"; Casual Game = "accessible, single-player, and designed for quick, engaging fun". — [update 40097](https://start-developer-competition.devpost.com/updates/40097-eyes-on-the-prize)

**2026 criteria (verbatim from the Devpost judging page):**
- Innovation & Creativity: "Measures originality, creative ambition, and relevance to the chosen track (Gaming, Entertainment, or Productivity). High-scoring projects differentiate themselves, show developer ingenuity, and uniquely leverage Meta device capabilities (e.g., spatial depth, hands-first design, gaze, passthrough mode, scene understanding, persistent anchors) to drive repeat usage."
- Experience Design: "Evaluates the quality of design, mechanics, and user journey tailored to the intended audience, emphasizing seated, hands-first, or specific context use. High-scoring entries ensure intuitive navigation from the moment of entry, clear onboarding, and a rewarding, habit-forming purpose. For entries that use passthrough, passthrough should be purposeful. The player's real room should meaningfully change the experience, whether a table becomes the play surface, walls host content, or UI adapts to the space, rather than a scene that plays identically with passthrough off."
- Technical Implementation: "Assesses technical expertise, reliability, and strategic use of platform tools, particularly the features that define hands-first experiences: gaze interactions (Unity/Unreal ISDK v207+), hand tracking, passthrough, spatial anchoring, and FoV-aware design. High-scoring entries must be performant on Meta Quest hardware (min 60 fps), solid and bug-free … For entries that use passthrough, this includes correct use of scene understanding (MRUK), anchoring content to detected planes, surfaces, and room geometry, and holding up in rooms the developer never tested."
- Polish & Presentation: "… high-quality submission materials (text, images, video) that effectively communicate the project's value. On your video/trailer: show real gameplay that honestly represents the experience. Lead with the story and what makes your project distinct. Don't lean on AI-generated video to carry the pitch."
- Judging process 2026: "a panel of experts that may include Meta employees and third parties. There will be two stages of judging." — [2026 judging](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/judging)
- 2026 FAQ on process: "first, all submitted projects are reviewed to ensure they meet the baseline requirements, including alignment with the competition theme, their selected track and division, and proper use of required tools and features. Projects that pass this initial check are then evaluated by a panel of expert Meta and third party judges according to four equally weighted criteria … Each criterion accounts for 25% of the total score … In the event of a tie, the judges will vote". — [2026 FAQ](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/faqs)
- 2026 hard requirements and guidelines (overview): "Hands-first: Your experience must be fully usable with hands, end-to-end"; "Seated-optimized: Design for seated, stationary use. Limit roomscale, no large physical movement"; "Fast cold start with 'something satisfying in 10 minutes or less'"; "Core experience is Entrant-built. Thin wrappers around existing platforms are discouraged"; easy pause/resume. — [2026 overview](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/)
- 2026 FAQ: "The test is simple: can someone complete the entire experience without ever pairing a controller?"; "Use of unreleased Meta tools, SDKs, or features is not permitted"; apps funded by Meta RFPs are ineligible; "Thin wrappers around existing platforms or services (YouTube, ChatGPT, etc.) are highly discouraged"; submissions as APK on a release channel named "Competition" "or to a GitHub page link (if built with IWSDK/WebXR)"; engines allowed: "Unity, Unreal, Godot, Native/Adroid, Immersive Web SDK or another engine. - 2026 special awards (verbatim descriptions): Best Social & Multiplayer Experience — "co-watching, cooperative play, social spaces, asymmetric multiplayer"; Best Agentic Interaction — "spatial agents, voice-first interaction, procedural content, context-aware assistance"; Best Reason to Come Back — "something to work toward, visible growth between sessions, a loop that pulls the user back tomorrow. - Meta Connect blog on what judges value: "A tight vertical slice is better than a broad prototype with gaps, because judges play what you submit."; experiences should be "seated-optimized" and "easy to get into/out of (fast cold start, clean pause/resume and something satisfying in 10 minutes or less)". — [Meta Connect 2026 blog](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/meta-connect-2026-vr-start-developer-competition/)
