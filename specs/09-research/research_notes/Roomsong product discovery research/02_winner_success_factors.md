# Winner success factors — Meta Start Developer Competition (2025 primary; 2024 Presence Platform Hackathon secondary)

Research notes for Roomsong (hands-first MR music garden on a table; Entertainment track, New Experience division, Meta VR Start Developer Competition 2026). Written 2026-10-10. Scope: WHY winners/finalists succeeded, grounded in official rubrics and the winners' own public material; what their demos, videos and Devpost pages did well; what Meta's 2026 materials emphasise.

Access notes (important for the report writer):
- Devpost project pages block plain fetches (HTTP 403). They were reached through the r.jina.ai reader using the trailing-slash URL form; each successful read is cited. The summaries below are extractions, not full transcripts, and word counts are the extractor's estimates (±20%).
- The following were NOT accessible and are marked as gaps: the 2025 project gallery (403), the 2026 Devpost discussion forum (returned navigation only), the Meta community-forum threads for the 2025 competition and the winners announcement (403), the Devpost page for the Hand Interactions winner "Hand Survivor" (slug unknown; web-search budget exhausted before it could be located), the Wayback Machine (blocked by the tooling), and the video frames themselves (only YouTube metadata — title, length, publish date, description, chapter list — could be read, so "first 20 seconds" analysis is limited to what descriptions/chapters state).

### Inferences
- Meta's winner one-liners are value propositions in ≤12 words that name the modality and the setting ("all you need is a pinch", "any room", "everything around you"). An entry whose tagline cannot be compressed to this form is at a communication disadvantage (analytical interpretation).
- Music/creative-audio concepts have placed in every recent Meta competition (Inkphony winner, (m)ORPH HM, OpenSoundLab Judges' Choice in 2025; Orbital Beats and Loop Jam in 2024), so "music in MR" is not disqualifyingly crowded but is a known pattern — differentiation must come from mechanics and the room, not the theme (analytical).

### Gaps
- No Meta blog, podcast or forum post with judges explaining individual decisions was found; the Meta community-forum winners thread returned 403.
- Meta's winners post gives no one-liners for special-award winners; those were reconstructed from Devpost pages where reachable.

### Inferences
- Pattern across reachable winners: (a) a one-sentence value proposition that names the input and the place; (b) a gesture vocabulary spelled out as verbs (pinch, tap, scrub, smack, clap, grab-and-throw); (c) at least one honest limitation or failed experiment; (d) a concrete scope statement (days, fps) in at least half the cases; (e) a public try-it link. None of this is required by the rules, so it reads as a shared signal of credibility (analytical).
- The shortest winning video (46 s, the Casual Game winner) suggests judges did not reward length; what the descriptions show is footage of the actual loop. Chaptered longer videos (DreamSpace) organise by feature. With no frame access, the "first 20 s" cannot be verified here (gap).
- Two of the three WebXR winners published their source on GitHub and all three gave live URLs; for a WebXR entry a working public link is the "build" and functions as evidence of working functionality (analytical, consistent with the rules' WebXR link requirement).

## Key Question 4 — What do Meta's 2026 materials emphasise (requirements, criteria text, special awards, webinars, design docs, gamer segments)? Concrete do/don't lists.

### Takeaway
2026 adds three hard framing requirements (hands end-to-end, seated, satisfying in ≤10 minutes), tightens the criteria text (passthrough must change the experience; honest footage; onboarding without walls of text; habit-forming purpose) and introduces special awards whose definitions are effectively a design brief for a tabletop music garden ("First Five Minutes", "Reason to Come Back", "Boldest Original Concept").

### Cited Findings
- Hard requirements (2026 overview): "Your experience must be fully usable with hands, end-to-end."; "Design for seated, stationary use. Limit roomscale, no large physical movement."; "Fast cold start, clean pause/resume, and something satisfying in 10 minutes or less." — [2026 Devpost overview](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/)
- Hands-first test (rules): "Fully usable with hands end-to-end. Controller support optional." … "Can someone complete the entire experience without ever pairing a controller?" — [2026 rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules)
- Stage One (2026) is pass/fail on whether the Project "reasonably fits the theme and reasonably applies the required tools and features featured in the Contest"; Stage Two is four criteria at 25% each — [2026 rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules)
- Criteria text (2026 judging page): Innovation & Creativity "Measures originality, creative ambition, and relevance to the chosen track" and unique leverage of "spatial depth, hands-first design, gaze, passthrough mode, scene understanding, and persistent anchors to encourage repeat usage". Experience Design: "intuitive navigation upon entry, clear onboarding, and a rewarding, habit-forming purpose"; for passthrough: "The player's real room should meaningfully change the experience, whether a table becomes the play surface, walls host content, or UI adapts to the space, rather than a scene that plays identically with passthrough off." Technical Implementation: minimum 60 fps on Quest, solid/bug-free, correct scene understanding (MRUK) usage and "robustness across untested environments". Polish & Presentation: UI/UX, art direction, sound design; "showing genuine gameplay in video submissions rather than AI-generated content" — [2026 judging](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/judging)
- Video rules (2026): "less than three (3) minutes", footage "as viewed on a Meta Quest device or via XR Simulator or another equivalent emulator"; must show "real gameplay that honestly represents the experience" and not rely "on AI-generated video to carry the pitch"; "AI-generated video misuse in presentation" is listed among disqualification grounds — [2026 rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules)
- New Experience division: "conceived of and built within the competition window (starting September 24, 2026). No pre-existing codebases, no shipped titles, no early access builds repurposed." — [2026 rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules)
- Special award definitions (verbatim): Best First Five Minutes — "The craft of the opening: how fast it explains itself, teaches controller-free interaction without a wall of text, and delivers a payoff before the headset comes off." Best Reason to Come Back — "Thoughtful progression and reward design: something to work toward, visible growth between sessions, a loop that pulls the user back tomorrow. Built for longevity, not a one-time demo." Boldest Original Concept — "Rewards the idea that couldn't exist anywhere else. A concept whose core loop depends on presence, spatial depth, real-world scale, or eyes and hands. Judged on originality and on whether the experience would fall apart on any other platform." Best Accessibility Forward — "voice alternatives for hand interactions, eyes-only navigation for limited mobility, high-contrast modes, inclusive by default."
