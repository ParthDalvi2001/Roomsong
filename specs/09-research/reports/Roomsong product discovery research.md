# Roomsong: Buy the Headset, Ship One Loop

**Product discovery report — Roomsong (Meta VR Start Developer Competition 2026, Entertainment track, New Experience division).** Prepared 2026-10-10 from eight research notes in `specs/09-research/research_notes/Roomsong product discovery research/`. Evidence conventions: every claim carries an inline link; **Confirmed** = stated by an official or primary source; **Inference** = our reading of confirmed facts; **Hypothesis** = untested proposal; **Unverified / Gap** = carried over from the notes unchanged. Promotional claims are reported as claims. Nothing in this report modifies project files.

Roomsong should be built exactly as a single, polished vertical slice — pinch a seed on a real table, hear it within a second, watch a tide of light play it, grow it, save it, come back tomorrow — with the **room signature** (room geometry → key/mode/reverb, shown by a tuning ripple) as the one feature that carries the Innovation score, and nothing else added until that slice runs at 72 fps on a Quest 3S in a stranger's room. The team genuinely needs a headset: the 2026 rules permit emulator footage but require a link "for judges to access and test your application", score Technical Implementation on "min 60 fps" on Meta Quest hardware, and reward entries that hold up "in rooms the developer never tested" ([2026 Official Rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules); [2026 judging page](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/judging)); the cheapest verified route is to buy a Quest 3S 128GB on Amazon.in (₹44,590 with next-day delivery shown on 2026-10-10) and resell it after 11 December ([Amazon.in search, observed 2026-10-10](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=meta+quest+3s)). The closest precedent to Roomsong, HandCraft XR: Winter Edition — a hands-only IWSDK/Three.js table diorama with local save, built in one month — won a $30k Judge's Choice in 2025, and three of the 32 2025 winners were music experiences, so the concept sits in a proven winning space whose only unoccupied slot is "the room tunes the music" ([HandCraft XR Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/handcraft-xr-winter-edition); [Meta winners blog, Dec 2025](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/meta-horizon-start-developer-competition-meet-the-winners/)). No shipped app on Quest, HoloLens, visionOS, iOS or Android was found that derives key, mode or tempo from room geometry, so the room signature is defensible as "first in a consumer MR instrument" (not "never done": MR4MR, ARCHISCORE and RoomTone exist as art, research or prototypes) ([MR4MR, arXiv 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07023); [ARCHISCORE, Villa Tugendhat 2021](https://www.vilatugendhat.cz/en/akce/interpretace-5-archipartitura/)). Before any device-dependent code is written, nine short proofs of concept (two emulator-only, seven on a borrowed or purchased headset) must settle plane semantic labels, persistent-anchor restore, pinch accuracy on a 16-step ring, frame rates, and Web Audio voice limits — none of which is documented for Quest Browser. The minimum reliable demo is plant → tide → grow → save → restore on a detected table with a working fallback when no table is found, recorded on a real Quest in under 90 seconds of real footage, hosted on GitHub Pages with a mirror and frozen at submission.

Three findings reshape the project's existing plan. First, the 2026 edition removed every engine-specific award (there is no IWSDK prize to fall back on), so a web entry competes head-on in Entertainment / New Experience, which has exactly two paid slots ($100k winner, $50k runner-up) plus six $25k special awards and two $20k Judge's Choice awards ([2026 Official Rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules)). Second, the Entertainment track is defined in 2026 as "lean-back media and content experiences — spatial cinema, interactive video, music visualization, immersive storytelling, spatial audio", and the 2025 Entertainment winner (DreamSpace) was an ambient, comfort-first room transformation rather than a game — so Listen mode is not a nice-to-have but the state that proves Roomsong belongs in its track ([2026 overview](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/); [DreamSpace Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/dreamspace-6g8rzm)). Third, the project docs place the bed centre 0.40 m ahead with a 0.30 m outer radius, while the design-evidence note recommends 0.45–0.55 m and ≤0.25 m; this report recommends 0.45 m (the top of the PRD's own 0.35–0.45 m range) with the radii unchanged until two device PoCs (pinch accuracy on the outer ring; VR-Glasses FoV simulation) decide between 0.30 m and 0.25 m (Deliverable 9).

### 5.2 Does Roomsong genuinely need a headset?

The honest split is this. Roomsong's **musical** value — a garden-sequencer loop with growth, a tide, a daily seed and no wrong notes — does not need a headset; Song Maker, Incredibox and Seaquence prove that loop on flat screens. Roomsong's **differentiating** value — a real table as the bed, free bimanual pinch in 3D, and room geometry shaping the music — needs passthrough, hand tracking and scene understanding, and nothing headset-free found in this research delivers that credibly to a consumer. The research on immersion is consistent with that split: a VR-vs-laptop film-music study found presence "fully mediates" the relationship between immersion and musical emotion; a 3D-sketching study (n=60) found VR raised stimulation, engagement and valence while 2D produced more unique features per idea; a science-lab study found VR produced "more presence but less learning" with higher cognitive load ([Abdou et al., CLLE](https://clle.univ-tlse2.fr/accueil/the-mediating-effect-of-presence-on-musical-emotions-in-virtual-environments); [Houzangbe et al., Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2022.958223/full); [Makransky et al. 2019 via iMotions](https://imotions.com/blog/adding-immersive-virtual-reality-to-a-science-lab-simulation-causes-more-presence-but-less-learning)). No study compares an MR music toy with its own screen version (gap), so the Devpost claim must be "immersion increases presence and engagement", not "our MR version is proven better".

For the competition the question is settled by the rules rather than by the research: a Quest or VR Glasses build is the entry; Meta's own competitions have never rewarded a headset-free entry (all 2023/2024/2025 winners are immersive MR apps), and a 2D panel/PWA would forfeit passthrough, hands and FoV criteria ([2024 winners blog](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/presence-platform-2024-digital-hackathon-winners-meta-quest/); [Meta 2D apps on Horizon Store](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/building-2d-apps-on-the-meta-horizon-store)). What is negotiable is **how much** headset time is needed. Emulator footage is explicitly permitted for the video ([2026 rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules)), and IWER 2.5 emulates hands with pinch, planes, meshes, hit-test, passthrough video and the VR-Glasses FoV mask ([IWER blog](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/immersive-web-emulation-runtime-iwer-webxr-meta-quest-developer/)). What cannot be validated without a Quest: real hand jitter over a real table (≈1.1 cm on Quest 3), passthrough legibility and contrast, plane semantic labels, persistent-anchor restore, the 60–72 fps budget, Web Audio latency, and the room signature end-to-end — which is why the room signature is the highest-risk feature to leave untested ([Godden et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40053653/); note 05 PoC list). Every reachable 2025 winner tested on device throughout; none reported building without one (note 02, factor 10).

Synthesis. Five-way uniqueness — hands-only + genuine table MR + legible position = time/pitch sequencer + persistence + room-derived musical parameters — holds against everything found; the closest two (Blooming Conductor, Sound Garden) each cover two of the five. The hands-only MR music apps that exist have ≤66 ratings or none, which judges may read as "novel" or as "niche"; present retention mechanics (daily seed, growth) to counter "niche". The first-minute experience should demonstrate the room signature before the garden metaphor (the tuning ripple sweeps the real table and walls, then the first seed lands already in the room's key), because "musical garden" alone reads as "Bloom on a table".

### 9.3 Rendering and audio budgets (derived; not official)

Rendering: ≤100 draw calls (the 80 cells and all plants as `InstancedMesh`), ≤150k triangles, one directional light, no shadow maps, no `EffectComposer`, MSAA through the XR layer's `antialias`, FFR medium, 72 fps target, `updateTargetFrameRate(90)` only if device frame time is under 9 ms — derived from Meta's statement that ~1,000 individual draw calls will fall below 72 fps and that render-target switches disable FFR and MSAA ([Meta perf workflow](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/webxr-perf-workflow/); [Meta perf best practices](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/webxr-perf-bp/)). HandCraft XR's own notes — standard materials and shadows "too heavy"; instancing, batching, BVH and adaptive shadows — corroborate the budget on this exact stack ([HandCraft XR](https://devpost.com/software/handcraft-xr-winter-edition)). Audio: native nodes on the real-time thread; a main-thread look-ahead scheduler (100 ms horizon, 25 ms tick) so tide timing never depends on the XR `requestAnimationFrame` loop; 24 equal-power panners with a custom distance curve; at most 1–2 HRTF "hero" sources if A/B on device justifies them; one shared convolver fed by a send bus with a ≤1.5–2 s procedural IR whose decay is set by the room signature's RT60; 16–24 voice cap with oldest/quietest stealing; `AudioContext` created or resumed inside the gesture that calls `requestSession`, with `resume()` again on session start and `visibilitychange`; `latencyHint: 'playback'` or ≈0.05 s only if PoC-W1 shows the Android-style burst glitch ([Adenot](https://padenot.github.io/web-audio-perf/); [web.dev scheduling](https://web.dev/articles/audio-scheduling); [Chrome AudioWorklet patterns](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/audio-worklet-design-pattern)). `AudioWorklet` does not require cross-origin isolation (only `SharedArrayBuffer` does), so GitHub Pages is compatible with a worklet-based scheduler; worklets are reserved for custom DSP if a Karplus-Strong pluck proves necessary.

### 9.4 Two decisions the notes left in conflict

**Bed geometry.** The PRD and REPLAN fix the annulus at 0.12–0.30 m with five rings 4.5 cm apart and the bed centre 0.40 m ahead (range 0.35–0.45 m; far edge ≤0.75 m reach cap) (`PRD.md`, `REPLAN_2026-10-10.md`). The design-evidence note recommends a ring-centre distance of 0.45–0.55 m and an outer radius ≤0.25 m, on three grounds: Meta's guidance that objects fixated for extended periods "should be rendered at least 0.5 meters away"; the VR-Glasses nominal FoV of 70°×66°, against which a 0.30 m outer edge at 0.40 m sits ≈37° off-axis (outside the 35° horizontal half-FoV) and ≈31° at 0.50 m; and Quest fingertip error that grows toward the periphery (1.3 cm peripheral vs 0.79 cm central on Quest 2) ([Meta Display](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/display/); [Meta FoV essentials](https://developers.meta.com/vr/essentials/field-of-view); [Abdlkarim 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830632/)). Two further facts bear on this. Shrinking the outer radius to 0.25 m with five rings reduces the radial pitch from 4.5 cm to 3.25 cm, eroding the margin the target analysis relies on (Park et al.'s 25 mm buttons with 5–9 mm spacing; Meta's "adjacent hit targets don't overlap") ([Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05833); [Meta hit targets](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/styles_inputs_hit_targets/)). And the 0.5 m rule concerns the eye-to-object distance: with seated eyes roughly 0.35–0.45 m above a table (assumption A4), the Euclidean eye-to-bed-centre distance at 0.40 m forward is ≈0.53–0.60 m, so the PRD value is not in conflict with Meta's comfort rule once the vertical offset is counted, although the user still looks down at the bed. **Recommendation:** set the default bed centre to **0.45 m** (the top of the PRD's own range, so FR-ROOM-03 is unchanged and the far edge stays at the 0.75 m cap), keep the radii at 0.12–0.30 m, and make the outer-radius question an explicit **E** item decided by PoC-H1 (outer-ring first-try hit rate ≥90%) and PoC-G1 (`metavr device fov-sim --perceived`, whole bed visible when looking at the centre). If either fails, reduce to 0.25 m and accept the 3.25 cm radial pitch with a 2.2 cm visible target, or keep 0.30 m and expect head turns. The REPLAN's "bed radius options → Cut" stands for the UI; the default is what changes.

**Hosting.** The 2026 rules say the WebXR link is "to your GitHub page" and the overview says "GitHub Pages link for WebXR projects", while the resources page lists both GitHub Pages and Vercel (with Meta's `iwsdk-v0-template`) ([2026 rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules); [2026 overview](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/); [2026 resources](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/resources)). The project's own rules digest lists GitHub Pages with Vercel as "an acceptable alternative" and the PRD defers the choice to Phase 1 (`HACKATHON_RULES.md`, `PRD.md` Q6). Technical facts: HTTPS is mandatory; GitHub Pages cannot set COOP/COEP headers, which matters only if `SharedArrayBuffer` is used; IWSDK's sub-path asset bug was fixed in July 2026; HandCraft XR submitted two hosts; the most common 2025 submission failure was "the wrong link" ([IWSDK ch.02](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/iwsdk/guides/02-testing-experience/); [GitHub Community #13309](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/13309); [issue #44](https://github.com/facebook/immersive-web-sdk/issues/44); [2025 update 40559](https://start-developer-competition.devpost.com/updates/40559-today-is-the-day-be-sure-to-submit-the-correct-url-to-your-project-on-your-devpost-submission)). **Recommendation:** submit a **GitHub Pages** URL (literal match to the rule text; free; frozen by tagging the deployment branch), served from a repository root or a custom domain to sidestep base-path issues (otherwise set Vite `base` to `/<repo>/` and verify with PoC-D1), and maintain a **Vercel or Netlify mirror** of the identical build hash as the second link in the Devpost description. Create the GitHub remote now: it also provides the New Experience division's practical proof (first commit on or after 2026-09-24), which the rules leave unspecified.

## 15. Final recommendation

**1. What to build.** A single hands-only vertical slice: the bed on the detected table (with a four-step fallback chain), pinch-to-plant with sound and sprout on the pinch, a 5-ring × 16-step pentatonic tide at 60–80 bpm, pull-to-grow / drag-to-move / drag-off-to-remove with undo, Listen mode, local save with a persistent anchor, a daily seed with visible growth, and the room signature (reverb RT60 from room volume; root/register from size; mode within consonant sets) made legible by a tuning ripple and a caption. All of it reachable by pinch alone, seated, within a 10-minute session, at 72 fps on a Quest 3S, served from GitHub Pages with a mirror. This is the loop the 2026 rubric describes almost verbatim — "a table becomes the play surface", "visible growth between sessions", "a payoff before the headset comes off" ([2026 judging](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/judging); [2026 special awards](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/special-awards)) — and the loop that HandCraft XR, Tiny Golf and Pocket Lands showed judges reward ([HandCraft XR](https://devpost.com/software/handcraft-xr-winter-edition); [Tiny Golf](https://devpost.com/software/tiny-golf)).

**2. What to avoid.** Controllers (optional under the rules and a dilution of the hands-first story); physics-driven hands or plants (Hand Physics Lab's 3.4★ despite 1.3M sales; FFR/MSAA cost); any second gesture family (LuminaMR's grab-vs-summon conflicts; Hand Survivor's "distinct enough" lesson); cloud AI or voice services (judging-time failure risk; wrappers discouraged; Nanauts hit experimental camera intrinsics in the browser); multiplayer (two 2025 winners disclosed colocation bugs); seasons, codex, hue twist, flick, image export; per-voice HRTF and multiple convolvers; post-processing and shadow maps; ceiling or wall planting; a 2D panel or PWA entry; claims of being the "first musical garden" (Blooming Conductor exists; Bloom: Open Space did pinch-in-air MR notes in 2018) ([Hand Physics Lab story](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/discover/success-stories/hand-physics-lab-holonautic/); [Lumina MR Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/negative-leap-second); [Microsoft News on Open Space](https://news.microsoft.com/features/brian-eno-peter-chilvers-create-quite-magical-flower-garden-sound-amsterdam-bloom-open-space/)).

**3. Highest-value features.** In priority order from the weighted table: the core pinch→sound→tide loop (4.30); the submission pack — device-recorded 60–90 s video and a Devpost page with "How to test" and "Known limitations" (4.25); the first-90-seconds onboarding (4.10); tracking-loss handling and hosting hygiene (3.90 each); table placement with fallback and the daily seed/growth loop (3.80 each). The room signature scores 3.40 only because its feasibility is gated on device PoCs; it is nonetheless the single feature that addresses Innovation and the Boldest Original Concept award and must not be cut.

**4. Do we genuinely need AR/VR glasses?** Yes. The entry must be a Quest/VR Glasses build; emulator footage is permitted for the video, but judges receive a link "to access and test your application", Technical Implementation is scored on "min 60 fps" on Meta Quest hardware, and Experience Design rewards scene understanding that holds up in rooms the developer never tested ([2026 rules](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/rules); [2026 judging](https://start-developer-competition-26.devpost.com/details/judging)). Five things cannot be validated without a device: plane semantic labels, persistent-anchor restore, pinch accuracy and jitter on a 4.5 cm cell pitch, the frame budget, and Web Audio latency/polyphony — and the room signature depends on the first two. Every reachable 2025 winner tested on device throughout. Minimum: one device day before 2026-10-24 and continuous access from Phase 2 onward. Recommended: buy a Quest 3S 128GB now (₹44,590 on Amazon.in, next-day delivery shown on 2026-10-10; ₹41,703 on Flipkart with an inconsistent listing), verify the seller and warranty in a browser first, and resell after 11 December; this costs about the same as two rentals from Ahmedabad shippers (3–7 working days each, deposits, unclear per-day units) and yields unlimited hours ([Amazon.in](https://www.amazon.in/s?k=meta+quest+3s); [Flipkart](https://www.flipkart.com/meta-quest-3s-128gb-vr-console-unmatched-gaming-high-resolution-display-lightweight-comfortable-design-128-gb-all-in-one-virtual-reality-experience/p/itm149b16ec18958); [Cyphon](https://www.cyphon.net.in/virtual-reality-box-for-rent.html)). The cost reasoning is ours, not a sourced comparison.

**5. Best alternative if not.** Build emulator-first with IWER 2.5 (hands with pinch, planes, meshes, hit-test, passthrough video, VR-Glasses FoV mask) plus a non-XR "garden window" render path in the same codebase, and script a one-hour session on a friend's headset: frozen staging URL, Web Launch QR via the phone, hand tracking and media sync enabled, record from the Navigator before "Enter XR", clips under a minute, fps overlay in-app because OVR Metrics CSV export is unavailable for browser content ([IWER blog](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/immersive-web-emulation-runtime-iwer-webxr-meta-quest-developer/); [Meta Web Launch](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/web-launch); [Meta Help: record video](https://www.meta.com/help/quest/516228089650875/); [Meta forum on OVR Metrics](https://communityforums.atmeta.com/discussions/WebXRDevelopment/ovr-metrics-tool-no-csv-recordings-for-webxr-application/1271550)). If the one-hour session is all the device time available, spend it on PoC-P1, P2, H1 and F1 in that order and ship the room signature as reverb + register only.

**6. Lessons from winners.** One verb taught by doing, with the table as the stage; a complete slice built in three to four weeks with features explicitly deferred to "What's next"; a short video that shows the loop in its first seconds and is honest about what it is; a named technical hook in "How we built it"; measured numbers (fps, build time); disclosed limitations; a public try-it link, ideally two hosts; a ≤12-word tagline that names the input and the place; and a write-up that lets a judge tick each criterion ([Meta winners blog](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/blog/meta-horizon-start-developer-competition-meet-the-winners/); note 02 factor table). None of them built without a headset.

**7. Differentiation.** Four claims, each true against everything found: the room tunes the music (geometry → key/mode/reverb) and shows you how (tuning ripple) — no shipped consumer app does this; the user authors the music on a legible ring where position is timing and pitch, rather than conducting pre-written songs; it is fully offline and procedural (Web Audio), in contrast with Inkphony's and Sound Garden's cloud pipelines; and the garden persists and grows without punishment. Phrase it as "first in a consumer MR instrument / first on Quest", citing MR4MR, ARCHISCORE and RoomTone as the art and research antecedents ([MR4MR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07023); [ARCHISCORE](https://www.vilatugendhat.cz/en/akce/interpretace-5-archipartitura/); [RoomTone](https://github.com/saagpatel/RoomTone)).

**8. Validate before coding.** In this order: PoC-E0 and PoC-D1 and U1 (emulator; this week); then on device PoC-P1 (labels), P2 (permission decline), H1 (pinch on the ring), F1/F2 (frame rates, FFR), W1 (audio), A1 (anchors), M1 (mesh), G1 (FoV sim) — about 3.5 hours on one device day; then the two design conflicts (bed centre 0.40 vs 0.45–0.55 m; outer radius 0.30 vs 0.25 m) settled by H1 and G1; and the hosting path (sub-path vs root/custom domain; SAB-free build). The perceived causality of the room signature (H13) is the one validation that needs strangers and two rooms, so it belongs in Phase 3, before the video is shot.

**9. Minimum reliable demo.** On a detected table (or via hit-test when there is none, or when permission is declined): enter with one pinch → ripple and room chord → ghost-hand pinch → first seed sounds within a second → tide plays → three more seeds harmonise → grow one, move one, remove one with undo → Listen for ten seconds → remove the headset → return and find the garden restored one growth stage older with a new seed. Hands-only end-to-end, 72 fps on a Quest 3S, zero console errors, recorded on device in under 90 seconds, hosted on GitHub Pages with a mirror, frozen and tagged before 2026-11-18 12:00 PT with the build hash in the Devpost text.

**10. Implementation order.** (1) Order the headset and open the GitHub remote, Pages and a mirror; run E0/D1/U1. (2) Skeleton with the mode gate, input abstraction, instanced bed and the garden window. (3) Device PoC day; decide geometry, anchors, `latencyHint`, frame rate. (4) Core loop: pinch detector → plant/tide/pentatonic/scheduler → grow/move/remove/undo. (5) Placement fallback chain, tracking-loss handling, hover cue, captions. (6) Save/restore + anchor, pause/resume, perf HUD, flashing cap, landing page. (7) Onboarding state machine. (8) Room signature + ripple. (9) Listen mode, daily seed/growth, accessibility toggles, palm menu, gaze dwell. (10) Stranger tests in three rooms; perf soak; hands-first audit. (11) Video, Devpost copy, freeze, submit on 2026-11-17; uptime checks until 2026-12-11. Should items that are not green by 2026-11-10 move to "What's next" in the Devpost text rather than into the build.
